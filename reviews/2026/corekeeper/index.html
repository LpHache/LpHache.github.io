Core Keeper

Score: 8/10

Playtime: 38 hours
Platform: PC
Storefront: Steam







Core Keeper is an open world survival crafting game similar to Minecraft and Terraria. This time however, you view the game from a top down perspective with pixel graphics and the game takes place in an alien world. There are multiple biomes and you harvest wood, minerals, food from enemies and the environment to upgrade you gear defeat a series of world bosses. The story consists of the "core" telling you which bosses need to be slain next. The rest of the game plays out like most other games of the genre.

Final Opinion

I have mixed feelings about core keeper. I started out really liking the game, because the first sessions of any survival crafting games always include tons of discovery and new items and just figuring out all the possibilities. I also had a great time base building, as I always do in this type of game. I also really settled into a magic and bombs playstyle for combat which resonated with me a lot. However, over time, a lot of frustrations came up. Biggest of which was gear progression. For some reason, standard melee fighting gear and armor was always available to craft at every tier of ressources that we found, without anything for other builds. For mage and explosive stuff, I had rely on finding loot chests or grinding bosses to get the pieces for the set I needed. Since none of these methods were very consistent and were RNG dependant, it became frustrating. This is also compounded with the fact that there were at most 1-2 armor sets for my builds, so I could go a long time without getting any gear progression at all. Since our base was already built out, there wasn't any other kind of progression besides combat stuff that really motivated me. This related to the fact that I also didn't keep base building for very long, my usual time sink. I think the 2D nature of this game really takes away a lot of the fun I get in building bases (I didn't enjoy it in terraria either). All the cool decoration elements were also RNG and dungeon dependant, and not craftable, which let to me just not caring to farm them. I will say the bosses were pretty fun though (besides the oozes) and had some more interesting play patters. Overall, I had fun playing with friends, but I'm not sure I'd revisit it again.

The Good

++++ Great gameplay variations. There's a lot of cool builds to do in this game. I went wizard and the staves I had access to were quite nice. My bomber set was also fun except for the friendly fire part. Each class had some nice weapons to play around with. +++ visual style is well done. The game looks really good, I enjoyed the designs for mobs and decorations and plants that they used a lot.
+++ Exploration is quite fun. The biomes are all pretty different, they have nice vibrants colors and a good variety of stuff to discover within them.
+++ Good boss fights. The boss fights were pretty interesting to do. They all looked cool and had some interesting mechanics. The challenge is made a bit trivial by respawning and teleporting back quickly in multiplayer but I prefer that to grinding them a lot.
++ Decent music and sound design. That is all.

The Bad

-- Gear progression was frustrating. You can't lock everyone's gear behind boss grind and rare RNG drops in chests. You also can't only make 2 sets across 20 levels and have huge gaps in between each, it just makes for a very slow progression.
-- Decorating and basebuilding RNG frustrating. Just like gear, you have to provide me with ways to craft the cool decorations after I've found them. I can't decorate if I have to rely on finding RNG decors.
-- Food was unintuitive. Getting good food for magic damage and mana regeneration was just unintuitive. Even when combining "glow" items they would sometimes just give crit damange and melee stuff. Very wiki dependant.
- Basic mob combat became repetitive. Every mob in the game you either run past, shoot while walking away, or hit twice in melee range. None of the mobs provided much of a challenge, and they were very reptitive in their respective biomes (fire butterflies in the desert were so annoying).
