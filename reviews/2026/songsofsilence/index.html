Songs of Silence

Score: 8/10

Playtime: 30 hours
Platform: PC
Storefront: Epic Games



Songs Of Silence is a turn-based strategy game in a similar genre as Heroes of Might And Magic (HOMM). You recruit units in your towns and send them out with heroes to conquer other towns. One major difference here though is that the battles are auto-battled with no manual input. I mostly just played the campaign, where you alternate between two factions and go through 8 predefined missions which are all pretty long in their own right. I was attracted to this game by a craving for a HOMM-like game and the unique art style it had that really reminded me a lot of the art of the graphic novel Kill 6 Billion Demons. You upgrade settlements with gold and upgrade materials (which are also generated by cities and gained from winning fights) up to 3 levels and heroes can add a max of 1 building to any settlement. There's no strict unit tiers but price does scale with power. I found the campaign of the game to be a pretty good time but I lacked depth to want to keep playing skirmishes (like one would do in Civ, HOMM, AOE, etc).

Final Opinion

I have truly mixed opinions on this game. On one hand, the campaign was actually pretty fun and well balanced. On the other, quick fights / skirmishes just lack a lot of compelling gameplay elements. You don't notice as much in the campaign since you have specific goals and understand that limited unit selection is a balance / lore reason for the mission. In skirmish though, just trying to win and getting units you know exist is a more frustrating endeavour since some buildings are only unlocked after you hero reaches a certain level and others depend on the type of settlement you control, and is never really explained. The other reason is the overworld is super barebones. There are no random events, no wandering travelers, no barbarian villages with random loot, no small ressource chaches.. nothing. There are only settlements and roaming neutral armies. It makes for pretty linear gameplay for a HOMM-style game if you ask me. In an RTS or pvp focused mode I would understand but for turn based stuff it makes it quite repetitive. I tried one game, got destroyed in 6 turns by the medium AI with a huge army and gave up. Anyways, beside that I do want to point out the awesome art style that is showcased throughout the game, it really caught my eye and looks great. I also really enjoyed the unique take on fantasy factions (no the usual elves or undead or demons or stuff like that). Theres the thousands kingdoms, the most classic mostly armed with knights, cavalry but also divine infantries and a celestial snake being. There are the Old Race, who specialise in upgrading civilians into warriors and mages, but also have a vast array of animated rocks called Konstructs that are pretty dope. Lastly there is the Crusade, which mixes corrupted plants, ancient void beings, and ominous konstructs. At the end of the day, I was debating between a 7.5 and an 8, settled around 8 since I did enjoy my time playing the campaign despite the other flaws. Would only recommend if you really love HOMM-style games and are craving a new experience, otherwise safe skip.

The Good

++++ Strong visual style with really cool art. I'm a sucker for good art and that really grabbed me for this game.
+++ Cool unique factions. Felt really different from stuff I'd played before and there was a really good selection of units.
+++ The campaign was well built. Good variety of objectives and map layout. It makes you switch between 2 factions as well.
++ Story is decent. Kept me interested and the ending was fun to play out.
+ Unlockable heroes. I enjoy the fact that a lot of hero unlocks for regular play are locked behind (simple) achievements. I know it bothers some people but I miss when games did that more.
+ The jury is still out on auto-battlers. It does make combat faster, but its also a lot less interactive. It's alright. You do have spells you can use mid combat though to help out.

The Bad

--- Overworld map is too barebones. Only settlements and armies. It lacks events and caches and map quests or anything.
-- Settlement upgrades and units paths are confusing. No simple upgrades or clear indication of how to obtain certain units.
- AI beat me up in 6 turns in PVE. How the hell, their army made no sense for turn 6.
- Why is everything a card. It's like it tried to intergrate the card game fad but it doesn't make sense here. There's no deck or random cards at all. They use it for spell casts and unit recrutement and overworld actions. Weird UI choice.
