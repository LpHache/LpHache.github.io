Viewfinder

Score: 9/10

Playtime: 5 hours
Platform: PC
Storefront: Epic







Viewfinder is a very simple puzzle game. The core principle is taking / finding pictures which you can then "apply" to any part of the world you are looking at, and it will be replaced by the contents of the image. For example, taking a picture of a staircase and "applying" it over the in-game hallway will completely replace it with a staircase at the point and orientation you chose. The game is seperated into a series of very small puzzle areas, each level being pretty much exactly 1 puzzle which leads into the next. Each of these levels can be found across 5 small hub worlds, each left behind by a scientist in particular. There is a story encompassing all of this, about scientists using a digital realm to more easily experiment and discover the secret to save Earth's climate. You are merely following through behind them, trying to figure out what they discovered before abandoning the realm.

Final Opinion

Viewfinder was a really good puzzle game in my opinion. The solutions to the puzzles were never too obscure or complicated and I never had to look up the solutions elsewhere (looking at you Talos Principle). At the same time, they required enough thinking for them to feel really satisfying to solve. It's a tough line to balance on but I think this game really nailed it. It's also a concept that I had never really played around with before and I found it pretty novel and fun. I deducted a point because the story was pretty bland and honestly distracted from the gameplay more than anything. The game was perfect length too sitting at 4-5h, enough to explore the mechanic fully but not get bored of it.

The Good

++++ Really cool core mechanic. Using a camera to override the game world with whatever picture you have was just super fun to play with. I found it interesting from a technical point of view too.
+++ No repeat levels. Part of the reason it has a tight playtime is because no 2 levels use the same solution. Each level introduces you to a new way of thinking or a new mechanic, meaning you're always thinking differently.
++ A cute talking cat. A cute talking cat follows you around. You can pet it.

The Bad

-- The story was just weird. There are notes and audiologs of the previous scientists everywhere around the levels. Most of them feel pointless to listen to, and the ending barely has anything to do with the story. Plus, they make you get "out" of the simulation twice and communicate with someone outside for like, no reason at all.
- Assets were a bit too "default"-y Felt like generic Unity assets for the most part. I understand having complicated visuals for this kind of game would be really hard, but a little more unique visual design would have been nice.
