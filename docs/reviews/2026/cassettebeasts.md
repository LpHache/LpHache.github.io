**Cassette Beasts**  is a creature collector game most similar to the well known Pokemon series. You get teleported to this mysterious world of New Wirral and are looking for a way back to Earth. To do so you learn to use tapes to capture the forms of monsters and turn into some yourself. There are plenty of ranger trainers (gym leaders) across to map to fight, 130 different monsters to find, and fights are up to 3v3. The story involves fighting strange otherworldly beings to unlock a way back to Earth. The graphics are pixel-style with a top down view with a map that brings you across plenty of different biomes. You're also accompanied by a companion at all times and you have a choice between a few of them. Easy game to pick up and play during breaks, and the collector in me had a great time filling out my beastiary (legally-distinct pokedex).<br/>

#### **Final Opinion**
I thought cassette beasts was actual a really fun little game. I came in with very low expectation since I got it for free on Epic and I barely knew anything about it. I only knew I always wanted to play more pokemon style games so I gave it a try. I quickly got hooked. I know the more "modern-day" looking monsters can put off some people but I thought they did a really good job making them looking pretty unique. It was interesting having new types that aren't common in this genre like Metal, Plastic, and Astral. I also really enjoyed the approach to type advantages. Instead of just bonus damage or damage resistance, type weakness instead applied unique buffs or debuffs depending on the situation (examples below) which made combat a lot more dynamic that just switching to the type-adv creature and one-shot criting. I also liked the approach to action-point style combat comparent to limited uses like pokemon has. Each turn you get 2AP by default and moves can cost more / less and its more interesting to manage that for big moves. Theres also a fusion system where you and your partner fuse your monsters into a combined form which shares the moves, types, and gains double AP. There are over 16k fusions possible all with a different name and look, its fun to use. The story wasn't particularly crazy, more a means to go around and explore the entire map (which is fine imo) but the combat and monster collection really kept me engaged. Would highly recommend if you're looking for a pokemon-style game.<br/>


#### **The Good**
**++++ The type advantage system was super dynamic**. Made fights more interacting and less one-sided. Examples: Water extinguishes fire (reduces melee and ranged att dmg), fire causes updraft on air (gives them a free air wall), plastic destabilises astral (makes them berserk, offensive moves only), ice flinches water (turns them into ice type, and stuns 1 round), lightning makes static with plastic (imbues their attacks with extra lighting dmg), etc..<br/>
**++++ Monster design was really fun.** I never felt like any of them in particular were rip offs of another franchise, the evolutions were neat, and collecting is fun.<br/>
**+++ Levels are tied to character, not monster.** The monsters always scale to your level, so new monsters stayed relevant even from earlier zones. Each monster could still be leveled from 1 to 5 stars to learn new moves (and evolve) to keep some progression.<br/>
**+++ The companions had good personalities.** There isn't a huge a mount of dialogue, but they all had a thing going for them and conversations at camp. Plus one of them is a dog. Woof.<br/>
**+++ There is actual post game content.** You can refight every gym leader who now have enhanced teams, you can more easily track down bootlegs (shinies - monsters but off-type), and train new recruits, and do the roguelike train.<br/>

#### **The Bad**
**-- I thought the overwold was a bit simplistic.** Yes there are many different biomes with different monsters, but that all kind of look like different shades of the same thing. Very few landmarks, there's only 1 town, and besides caves and 1 mall there's not that much more to the overworld besides story areas.<br/>
**- I wish there were longer fights in the base game (before endgame)** Most trainers only had half a roster, there was only 1 fight with a full roster enemy.<br/>
